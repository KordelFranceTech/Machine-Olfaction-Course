# Editorial boundaries and maintenance

The course is a standalone university engineering resource grounded in public science. Its organizing objective is to help learners derive a complete sensing architecture, evaluate its evidence, and develop useful independently documented extensions.

## Content boundaries

Learner-facing material belongs in `docs/`. Use independent public papers, official terminology, documented component interfaces, public metrology resources, and clearly labeled original teaching examples. Keep confidential formulations, product-specific operating parameters, unpublished validation data, internal algorithms, customer information, and restricted third-party material outside the course.

Do not treat a publicly readable document as automatic permission to reproduce its text, figures, code, or data. Use short original explanations and link to the evidence. Preserve different reuse terms for articles, data, software, and hardware. No external dataset or third-party figure is bundled in this edition.

## Scientific style

State units, reference conventions, assumptions, and measurement conditions. Label synthetic values and distinguish calculations from observations. Separate established physical relationships, engineering approximations, research demonstrations, interpretations, and proposals. Include failure modes and tests that challenge the preferred explanation.

Do not convert a materials observation into demonstrated selectivity, a fit into a unique mechanism, a random data split into deployment evidence, or a chemical association into clinical usefulness. Keep reported performance within the scope of the cited experiment. Mark abstract-level evidence when full methods were not reviewed.

## Independent-project boundaries

The derivative projects are technical candidates with unassessed legal clearance. Their public provenance and architectural differences do not establish noninfringement. Follow the learner-facing [responsible-innovation guide](docs/responsible-innovation.md) to prepare concrete design records for appropriate review. Keep legal opinions and confidential review materials separate from educational publication.

## Updating the course

For each revision, record the changed lesson, scientific reason, source and access date, affected calculation, and any downstream design implication. Check corrections and retractions for key papers. Refresh the research-frontier module with substantive advances rather than adding recent dates without a teaching purpose.

When changing a numerical example, rerun the corresponding code and update expected output and solutions together. When changing a filename or heading, check inbound links. When adding a new source, preserve its title, authors, year, stable link, relevance, evidence limit, and access basis in the appropriate bibliography.

Use the central curriculum and design studio as the consistency checks: every substantive lesson should support an engineering decision, and every design decision should have a verification path. A new module that duplicates existing coverage may be less useful than a better worked example or a stronger independent validation exercise.

## Release record

Record content revision and research checkpoint separately from a future hosting deployment date. Keep the publishing repository limited to intended course material. Website access must be checked on the deployed site, not inferred from repository privacy. See [PUBLISHING.md](PUBLISHING.md).
